Local only lets a guest into your Venue once it has detected they are physically there. Three technologies can do that detection. You pick one — the setup wizard recommends a fit when you sign up, but the choice is yours.

Until one is active, nobody can enter your Venue.

## The three options

  <Card title="Bluetooth beacons" icon="bluetooth">

  </Card>
  <Card title="WiFi network" icon="wifi">

## Which one fits

| | Beacons | WiFi | GPS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Best for** | Bars, cafés, restaurants, shops — anywhere without managed network gear | Hotels, multi-room or distributed venues with managed access points | Resorts, campgrounds, remote B&Bs, open-air events |
| **Precision** | Room-level, and can gate individual zones | Per access point | Site-level boundary |
| **Needs existing infrastructure** | No | Yes — managed APs | No |
| **You can set it up yourself** | Yes | No | No |
| **Cost** | Paid hardware | No hardware | No hardware |
| **How to get it** | Order through the setup wizard | Contact Local support | Contact Local support |

<Tip>
Beacons are the only option that supports **gated zone rooms** — chat rooms that open only for guests standing near a specific beacon. If you run a large Venue and want to drive traffic to a particular area, that is an argument for beacons even where WiFi would otherwise do.
</Tip>

## Beacons

Beacons are paid hardware. There is currently a promotion offering a free beacon to approved requests — ask during signup or contact support.

**How many you need:** one or two for a small Venue, three for a larger one. The setup wizard recommends a number based on what you tell it about your space.

**What you receive:** USB-powered or battery-powered iBeacons, unconfigured from the factory.

### The lifecycle

<Steps>
  <Step title="Order">
    Through the setup wizard at signup, or by contacting support later.
  </Step>
  <Step title="We ship">
    Local adds the beacons to your Venue profile and marks them shipped. They appear in the app's device management section before they reach you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="You configure them">
    Open device management in the app. It lists the beacons you should have received. Tap the scan button — the app finds them, connects, and writes the correct identifier and a protected password to each one.

    If several beacons are in range, all of them are configured in a single pass.
  </Step>
</Steps>

That is the whole setup. Two steps in the app, no technical knowledge, no pairing codes to type.

## WiFi

If you run managed access points, they can serve as the presence signal: Local recognises your Venue from the access point a guest is connected to.

This is not self-serve. Contact Local support and we will configure it against your network.

<Note>
Guests are also offered a one-tap WiFi profile install during onboarding, which lets them connect to Local-enabled networks automatically on future visits.
</Note>

## GPS

A geofence around your site. Suited to large, isolated locations where a guest being "within the boundary" genuinely means they are at your Venue.

Not self-serve — contact Local support. Your address polygon was already pulled from OpenStreetMap at signup, so there is usually a boundary to start from.

<Warning>
GPS is a poor fit for a Venue on a busy street or in a shopping centre, where the boundary would also catch people walking past or sitting in the business next door. Use beacons or WiFi there.
</Warning>

## Checking it works

Once presence is live, the fastest test is to walk into your Venue with a phone that is **not** signed in as an admin. A guest account should move from the lobby screen to your Venue's welcome screen within a few seconds.

Your admin account will not test this correctly — admins bypass presence verification by design.
